Accessibility

Accessibility is the product.

Meet My Menu is designed around listening, speaking, and screen-reader navigation—not as a visual app with accessibility added later.

Audio first

Important guidance is spoken. The scanning flow tells users how to position a menu, and results are structured to be heard rather than merely seen.

Screen-reader readable

Controls use meaningful labels, headings follow a useful order, and the experience is built to work with VoiceOver and keyboard navigation.

Honest uncertainty

When a source is partial, unclear, or unavailable, the product should say so. A confident-sounding answer is not useful when the underlying menu does not support it.

About allergens: Meet My Menu’s job is to bring possible concerns to the diner’s attention. It surfaces the ingredients and allergen information it finds so the diner knows what to ask about, then confirms it with restaurant staff.

Tested beyond checklists

Automated accessibility checks are useful, but they are not the finish line. Real device and screen-reader testing is part of evaluating whether a flow actually works.

Help improve it

If you are a blind or low-vision diner, your experience can expose problems an automated audit cannot.
